# Zetic

An auxetic hand-rehabilitation device, developed during the Objects course of the Master of Design and Innovation program under the guidance of Joaquín Rosas, Master in Design for Emergent Futures at the Institute for Advanced Architecture of Catalonia (IAAC), and produced at the Fabrication Laboratory within designLab UAI.

The work focused on a 4×4 rotating-squares metamaterial lattice, printed on a Bambu Lab X1 Carbon in flexible TPU 90A, providing graded resistance for hand-therapy exercises through biaxial collapse across thin flexure hinges.

The mechanism lives in the material itself. The rotating-squares geometry has a negative Poisson's ratio — compressed along one axis, it contracts along the other — with the motion carried entirely by thin, precisely dimensioned hinges between rigid cells. The design supports finger flexion, fist closure, metacarpophalangeal (MCP) and proximal interphalangeal (PIP) flexion, thumb flexion, gross and cylindrical grip, thumb-to-index and tripod pinch, straight fist, and tendon gliding, among others.

Material selection was tested across TPU hardness grades: Shore 95A HF for fast-printing prototype iterations, TPU 90A for the final part, for its flexibility and elastic recovery under repeated compression.
